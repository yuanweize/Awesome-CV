# Job-specific positioning without changing the career story

Use for every JD, before writing the CV header or cover letter. Optimise for earning a
credible interview: make relevant work immediately legible, use the strongest supported
wording, and avoid both generic breadth and unnecessary self-disqualification.

## Three distinct titles

- `target.title`: the employer's exact vacancy title, retained for tracking and the CL
  subject such as “Application — Senior Platform Engineer”. This is not a claimed rank.
- CV headline: one coherent, evidence-supported professional focus matching the dominant
  duties. Usually 3–7 words; optionally one short adjacent specialism. Remove the broad
  multi-domain tagline when it dilutes the application.
- CL personal header: use the same chosen CV headline. The opening and proof should
  support that identity; never copy the Golden's broad default tagline into the CL.

Matching the employer's title is fine when the actual work supports it; copying is not
the objective. Do not inherit Senior/Lead/Principal, managerial ownership or regulated
qualifications from a vacancy. Do not automatically add Junior, Aspirant, or “seeking a
role” when a neutral professional focus is truthful. Do not rewrite historical job titles.

## Choose the strongest defensible focus

Read the daily responsibilities first. Select the best approved Pack by represented
evidence, then choose its focused headline. For example, infrastructure operations may
support “Linux Systems Engineer”; backend/service integration may support “Software
Integration Engineer”; field commissioning may support “Field Service Engineer”; an
embedded interface role may support “Embedded Systems Integration Engineer”. These are
examples, not universal aliases or permission to claim every such capability.

Map the proposed headline to two or three concrete evidence points when available, and
test whether the candidate could explain their work and its boundaries in an interview.
Adjacent experience can support a stretch application. A missing preferred tool or an
employer's aspirational years requirement is not automatically a rejection. Distinguish
trainable gaps from genuine mandatory eligibility or independent-ownership requirements.

Use specific actions and systems instead of unsupported adjectives. Personal/open-source
and AI-assisted work can be substantial proof of delivery: describe requirements,
integration, review, testing, deployment and operation when supported. Do not convert
repository languages into independent mastery or personal operation into enterprise duty.
Keep gaps in internal analysis unless a direct question or truthful interpretation needs
disclosure. Do not lead a letter with apologies, exhaustive caveats or a technology list.

## Small application-scoped edit

Default to a headline-only CV derivative and targeted CL. Preserve the existing CV body,
layout, project order and full Portfolio. Broader whitelisted changes are optional only
when a specific relevance defect warrants them; never redesign on receipt of a JD.
Reuse an already suitable title instead of forcing variation for every application.

Follow `application-variants.md`: use the reviewed presentation catalog or the legacy
title-only catalog. An alias absent from the catalog needs a focused semantic check
against current eligible claims; record that review and its claim fingerprints in the
private catalog, then generate the requested local draft. This is not approval of a new
Golden and does not require repeated permission for an authorised local draft. If the
evidence does not support the alias, choose the nearest supported focus and explain why
in internal analysis. Do not silently claim that an unchanged broad title was tailored.

Record in analysis: employer title, selected Pack, chosen CV/CL headline, evidence,
reason for any difference from the vacancy, and internal risks. Check the rendered CV
and CL headers for consistency, length and clipping; never claim a PDF is visually
reviewed based only on its text or a successful build.
