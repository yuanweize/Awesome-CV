# Historical CV audit

Use historical CVs to discover forgotten facts and repeated modelling failures, never
as factual authority. The audit has two passes:

1. **Blue recovery:** identify facts that may be real but are absent, over-compressed,
   linked to the wrong role family, or stranded in human-readable memory without a
   claim ID.
2. **Red challenge:** reject copied inflation, unsupported ownership, stale plans,
   mutable scale, invented seniority, and technologies that were only installed,
   explored, or generated by an agent.

Run the read-only inventory against ignored profiles and closed application archives:

```bash
./cv legacy-audit
./cv legacy-audit --extra-pdf meta/old-cv.pdf
```

Markdown and optional JSON output must use different paths under ignored
`meta/audits/` or `workspace/tmp/`. They are atomically replaced with owner-only
permissions. An explicitly requested `--extra-pdf` is mandatory: a missing, unsafe,
unreadable, or textless PDF fails the audit instead of disappearing from the inventory.

Report schema 1.1 keeps the two decisions independent:

- **Blue mapping:** token similarity to an eligible verified/self-reported claim is
  `covered` at 0.48 or above, `partial` from 0.22, and otherwise `unmapped`. Unmapped
  rows expose no best claim in Markdown; JSON retains the nearest match only for
  diagnostics. These thresholds indicate triage confidence, not factual proof.
- **Red governance:** every risky term is evaluated even when a sentence maps to a
  claim. It is `governed` only when an explicit master exclusion, role/claim/skill
  boundary, or planned/ineligible record covers that same risk. Merely listing a
  technology or having a weakly similar claim does not govern inflated ownership.

The tool extracts summaries, bullets, skills, entries, and honors; redacts direct
contact data; maps wording heuristically; and flags strong-language or scope risks. A
high unmapped count is a triage signal, not proof that the mother memory is incomplete.

Classify every material candidate as one of:

- **confirmed omission:** independent evidence or a fresh owner confirmation supports
  a new atomic claim;
- **weak modelling:** a true fact exists but is bundled, linked to the wrong role, or
  absent from an evidenced skill group;
- **needs confirmation:** historical wording identifies a useful lead but no current
  authority establishes scope, date, metric, or ownership;
- **unsupported inflation:** retain a boundary or exclusion so it cannot return;
- **obsolete or low-signal:** keep in lifetime history with `cv_eligible: false` and a
  reason when the owner still wants it remembered.

For official education records, compare course completion and grades with the
transcript. Course catalog pages may support syllabus topics but do not prove that a
student completed the course. For projects, inspect authored code and limitations.
For personal work, require fresh owner confirmation and explicit personal scope. For
employment or contracts, require the underlying record before strengthening ownership
verbs.

After accepting a confirmed omission:

1. add or reuse evidence;
2. create one atomic claim per independently reusable fact;
3. link role families and evidenced skill groups;
4. classify the corresponding human-readable history item;
5. validate the master and rerun the historical audit;
6. review whether the new claim materially changes a baseline or target-role
   recommendation.

Never lower matching thresholds or broaden a claim merely to make the audit statistics
look better. The objective is a more truthful model, not a smaller unmapped number.
